## 1. What we observed

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SD1.1 | 0.948 | 0.979 | −0.031 | 0.972 | 0.981 | −0.009 |
| SD1.5 | 0.949 | 0.978 | −0.029 | 0.946 | 0.963 | −0.017 |
| SD2.1 | 0.975 | 0.994 | −0.019 | 0.992 | 0.995 | −0.003 |
| KD2.1 | 0.977 | 0.999 | −0.022 | 0.982 | 0.999 | −0.017 |
| **MJ4** | **0.892** | 0.999 | **−0.107** | **0.484** | 0.997 | **−0.513** |
| **MJ5** | **0.883** | 0.997 | **−0.114** | **0.496** | 0.993 | **−0.497** |
| **MJ5.1** | **0.862** | 0.996 | **−0.134** | **0.440** | 0.989 | **−0.549** |

Two distinct effects are visible, and they need two distinct explanations:

- **A uniform, modest gap (~0.02–0.03 AP) on every generator**, including the ones that reconstruct via their *own* matching AE (SD1.1/1.5 → SD1, SD2.1 → SD2, KD2.1 → its own VQ-VAE). Since AP is threshold-independent, this can't be a calibration artifact — something about the whole real/generated separation shifted slightly.
- **A large, MJ-specific gap** (AP down ~0.11–0.13, TPR@5%FPR roughly *halved*) that the other four generators don't share at all.

Given you've confirmed the generated images themselves are unmodified copies of the paper's source data, the search narrows to the **reconstruction pipeline and the calibration data**, not the generated images. Below are the candidate causes, ranked by how well each one's mechanism matches the *specific shape* of the result (uniform-small vs. MJ-only-large).

---

## 2. Why it's very unlikely to be a code bug

Ruling this out first, because it constrains everything else: `src/config.py`'s `LPIPS_LAYER_INDEX = 1` is correct (paper's LPIPS₂), `AE_CONFIGS` loads all three AEs with the right `type` ("kl" vs "vq"), and `evaluate.py`'s `_paper_entry()` substring-matches your folder names to the right paper row. If the layer index or an AE mapping were wrong, the damage would show up **uniformly across all seven generators** (as it did in your earlier Kaggle run that used `LPIPS_LAYER = 2` by mistake). Instead we see four generators landing close to the paper and three landing far below — a *selective* pattern, which points at something that specifically touches Midjourney's detection pathway: the SD2 autoencoder and/or the real-image calibration set.

## 3. Ranked hypotheses

### 3.1 SD2 VAE weights are not the exact ones the paper used (primary suspect for the MJ-specific gap)

**Mechanism.** Per the paper (Sec. 5.2, Discussion): *"images generated by Midjourney can be detected almost perfectly by using the AE from Stable Diffusion 2"* — Table 2 shows 99.7–99.9% of MJ4/MJ5/MJ5.1 images have their *minimum* reconstruction error come from the **SD2 AE specifically**, not SD1 or Kandinsky. MJ's detectability is therefore not really about Midjourney at all — it's entirely riding on how well *this one specific AE* happens to generalize to MJ's output distribution. That's a much more fragile alignment than SD2.1-generated images being reconstructed by their own AE (which will look good under almost any reasonable checkpoint of the same architecture family, since the images were *produced by* that VAE's latent space in the first place).

Our `AE_CONFIGS["sd2"]["repo_id"]` is `sd2-community/stable-diffusion-2-1` — a **third-party re-upload**, not `stabilityai/stable-diffusion-2-1` directly. Its own model card states: *"This repository is a mirror of the now deprecated `stabilityai/stable-diffusion-2-1`, this repository and organization are not affiliated in any way with Stability AI."* Two things follow from that:

- It's a self-described mirror, not one verified or maintained by Stability AI — "mirror" is a claim, not a guarantee of bit-identical weights. Re-uploads of gated models are frequently re-serialized (safetensors conversion, fp16↔fp32 rounding, EMA vs. non-EMA weight selection) in the process, any of which can leave the VAE numerically close-but-not-identical to the original.
- **`stabilityai/stable-diffusion-2-1` is no longer on Hugging Face at all** — confirmed by hand, not merely gated behind a license click-through. Stability AI pulled it entirely at some point after the paper was published (2024), so there is no path — token, license acceptance, or otherwise — to fetching the original artifact anymore. Whatever the paper's authors ran against in 2024 is now unrecoverable in its original form.

**Why it fits the pattern.** It explains why MJ is uniquely hit while SD2.1 itself is barely affected (SD2.1's own generations don't need the generalization property to work), and why SD1.1/1.5/KD2.1 are unaffected (they don't route through the SD2 AE for their best reconstruction).

**Status: untestable directly, not ruled out.** Since the ground truth is gone, this can't be confirmed or eliminated by direct comparison. Two weaker options remain:

1. **Cross-check independent mirrors.** Search HF for other community re-uploads of the SD2.1 VAE (there are usually several — e.g. bundled inside other SD2.1-based pipelines/forks) and diff their `vae/diffusion_pytorch_model.safetensors` SHA256 against `sd2-community`'s. Agreement across multiple *independent* uploaders is reasonable (not conclusive) evidence the weights are faithful to the original, since it's unlikely several unrelated people converted the checkpoint in exactly the same lossy way.
2. **Indirect behavioral check.** SD2.1's own VAE, whatever its exact provenance, should reconstruct SD2.1-*generated* images almost perfectly regardless of minor weight drift (that's the "generated images live on the AE's manifold" property from Sec. 4 of the paper). Our SD2.1 AP (0.975) is close to the paper's (0.994) but not identical — if this AE were substantially different from what the paper used, that number would likely be more visibly off too. Its near-match is weak evidence *against* major weight drift, though it doesn't rule out the smaller kind of drift that would specifically hurt the fragile MJ cross-generalization case rather than SD2.1's own confident case.

Given this hypothesis can no longer be tested directly, **deprioritize it** relative to §3.2 and §3.3, which are fully testable with what you have.

**Fix.** None available — the reference artifact is gone. If independent-mirror cross-checking (option 1) turns up a mismatch, switch to whichever mirror best agrees with the majority; otherwise, treat the current `sd2-community` weights as the best available proxy and focus remediation effort on §3.2/§3.3 instead.

### 3.2 Stochastic latent sampling amplifies error on borderline cases

**Mechanism.** `src/reconstruct.py:28` (and the Kaggle worker) does:
```python
latents = vae.encode(image_tensor).latent_dist.sample()
```
`.sample()` draws a *random* sample from the encoder's posterior (mean + noise·std), not the deterministic `.mode()`/mean. This means every reconstruction — and therefore every Delta_Min score — has run-to-run noise baked in, on top of the "true" reconstruction error. For images that reconstruct very well under the *matching* AE (SD1.1 under SD1, SD2.1 under SD2), the posterior variance is small and the injected noise barely moves the score. For MJ images, which only reconstruct passably well via a *non-matching, cross-model* AE, the posterior is less peaked, so the same sampling noise perturbs the score more — pushing a meaningful fraction of already-marginal MJ images across the decision threshold in either direction, and adding avoidable variance to AP.

**Why it fits the pattern.** Same shape as 3.1: hits the fragile cross-generalization case (MJ→SD2) much harder than the confident matching-AE case, without needing any bug.

**How to verify.** Re-score a subset (e.g. 200 MJ images) using the deterministic mean instead: `vae.encode(x).latent_dist.mode()` for `AutoencoderKL` (VQModel's `encode()` is already deterministic — no change needed there). If AP/TPR jumps, this is a contributing factor.

**Fix.** Switch `.sample()` → `.mode()` in `reconstruct()` for the `"kl"` branch, in both `src/reconstruct.py` and the Kaggle worker scripts. This is also the more standard choice for a *reconstruction-fidelity* metric — sampling is meant for the generative use case, not for measuring how well an AE can represent a given input.

### 3.3 Real-image calibration set differs from the paper's (explains the uniform ~0.02–0.03 AP gap, and worsens MJ's TPR specifically)

**Mechanism.** The paper's real set (Sec. 7.1): 1,000 LAION-Aesthetics images, aesthetic score ≥ 6.5, min side ≥ 512px, ≤768² pixels, center-cropped to 512×512. Our `data/real/` (via `src/download_real.py`) uses the *same filter recipe* but pulls from **different, and more, source rows** (`real_metadata.parquet` + `laion_aesthetic_extra_shard0.parquet`, 1,024 images vs. the paper's 1,000) — a different random sample from the same population, not the same 1,000 images.

Even under identical filtering, the paper's own Sec. 5.4 finding — reconstruction error correlates with image complexity for real images — means *which* 1,024 real images you happen to draw measurably shifts the real-score distribution (more/less complex images = higher/lower Delta_Min). Since AP is computed against this real distribution for **every** generator, a harder real set drags AP down for all seven models roughly equally — which matches the ~0.02–0.03 uniform gap we see on SD1.1/1.5/2.1/KD2.1.

**Why it disproportionately wrecks MJ's TPR@5%FPR specifically.** The 5%-FPR threshold is the 5th percentile of *our* real scores (0.0168, per your `calibrate_target_fpr.py` output). Figure 3 / Figure 10h in the paper already show Midjourney's Delta_Min distribution is the **most spread out and closest to the real distribution** of all seven generators ("variants of Stable Diffusion and Kandinsky yield more compact distributions than Midjourney" — Sec. 8.3). MJ's mean score in our data (0.0173–0.0192) sits right on top of that 0.0168 threshold. A metric this close to the decision boundary is extremely sensitive to exactly where the threshold lands — a small shift in the real-score percentile (from a different real sample) moves a large fraction of MJ's probability mass across the line, while SD1.1/SD2.1/KD2.1 (whose generated-score clusters sit much further from the threshold, per Fig. 3) barely notice the same shift. This is a textbook explanation for why **AP dropped moderately but TPR@5%FPR collapsed**: TPR@threshold is a step function of exactly where the cut falls, AP is a smooth rank statistic.

**How to verify.** Recompute the threshold using only the *first* 1,000 images from `data/real/` (to match the paper's count) and check whether MJ's TPR@5%FPR moves closer to the paper's, even holding everything else fixed.

**Fix.** None strictly needed — this isn't a bug, it's an artifact of evaluating on a different (larger, independently-sampled) real set than the paper's fixed 1,000. If you want numbers directly comparable to Table 1/3, either (a) subsample your real set to the paper's original 1,000 images if you can identify them, or (b) report your own numbers as "evaluated on an independently-sampled real set of comparable size/filtering," which is a legitimate and common thing to state in a reproduction study — just not a strict apples-to-apples comparison.

### 3.4 Lower-priority / verify-only items

- **LPIPS backbone weights.** Your Kaggle logs show `torchvision` warning that `pretrained=True` for VGG16 now maps to `VGG16_Weights.IMAGENET1K_V1` by default. The `lpips` package pins its own linear calibration on top of a specific torchvision VGG16 checkpoint; if a future torchvision/lpips version silently changed which ImageNet weights get loaded, LPIPS distances would shift slightly for *every* image, real and generated alike. This would contribute to (not explain) the uniform small gap in §3.3, not the MJ-specific one. Worth pinning `torch`/`torchvision`/`lpips` versions explicitly and confirming against the paper's environment if you want to fully rule this out.
- **Image provenance/subset selection.** You've confirmed the generated images are unmodified copies of the paper's source data, so this is likely not a factor — but if any doubt remains, verify per-folder image count (paper used exactly 1,000 per model) and that no resizing/re-compression happened between download and use.

## 4. Recommended diagnostic protocol (standard practice for isolating a metric regression)

Change **one variable at a time**, re-score only the affected slice (few thousand images, cheap on the dual-T4 setup you already have), and check whether the MJ gap narrows:

`stabilityai/stable-diffusion-2-1` being gone entirely (not just gated) removes the cleanest test (§3.1) from the table, so the protocol now leads with what's actually testable:

1. **Isolate the sampling variable first** — switch `.sample()` → `.mode()` in `reconstruct()` (§3.2). Re-score MJ4/MJ5/MJ5.1 only (3,000 images) against the existing real scores. This needs no external dependency and is the highest-value test now available.
2. **Isolate the real-set variable** — recompute `choose_threshold_at_fpr` using only 1,000 of your 1,024 real images (fixed subsample), holding the sampling method from whichever performed best in step 1. Check whether TPR@5%FPR for MJ moves, and whether the uniform ~0.02–0.03 AP gap on SD1.1/1.5/2.1/KD2.1 narrows too — that's the signature to look for if this is really the driver of the uniform gap.
3. **Weak, indirect check on the AE weights (§3.1)** — cross-check `sd2-community`'s VAE checksum against any other independent SD2.1-VAE re-upload you can find on HF. Not conclusive, but agreement across independent sources is the only remaining signal available now that the original artifact is gone.
4. Report the numbers from steps 1–2 side by side against the baseline (this table, at the top of this doc) — that's the standard "ablation table" format for a reproduction discrepancy write-up, and will show which factor (or combination) accounts for how much of the gap, rather than leaving it as an unranked list of hypotheses.

## 5. Bottom line

- The uniform ~0.02–0.03 AP gap across **all** generators is most likely explained by evaluating against a different (larger, independently-drawn) real-image calibration set than the paper's fixed 1,000 — not a bug. This is directly testable (step 2 above).
- The large, MJ-specific collapse is best explained by Midjourney's detectability being **entirely dependent on a fragile cross-model generalization property** (SD2's AE happening to reconstruct MJ output well) that the paper itself flags as its own edge case (Fig. 10h, Table 2). Of the two things that stress that fragile property, stochastic `.sample()` reconstruction noise (§3.2) is fully testable and should be run first; the non-official SD2 VAE checkpoint (§3.1) is a real possibility but, with the original artifact now removed from Hugging Face entirely, no longer directly verifiable — treat it as an open, deprioritized suspect rather than something to chase further.
